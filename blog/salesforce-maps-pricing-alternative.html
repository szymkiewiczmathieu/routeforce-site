When pricing becomes the real blocker

Teams may evaluate a Salesforce Maps pricing alternative when the commercial model becomes harder to defend at the planned licensed team size.

On 5 September 2026, Salesforce’s US pricing card listed Maps at $75/user/month and Maps Advanced at $125/user/month, billed annually; an FAQ on the same page said $150 for Advanced. Verify current edition pricing directly with Salesforce. For a 20-person field team, the pricing-card figures equal $1,500 and $2,500 per month before Salesforce platform licences.

Per-user pricing turns route planning into a recurring budget debate. Every new user can reopen the question.

Why pricing model matters as much as product fit

If a route planning product is licensed to only part of the field team, compare that scope with the operating model leadership expects.

Compare the licence formula, included workflow, add-ons, implementation, and renewal terms beside the list price.

A public per-user rate is easy to model

Tourvia costs €30 excluding tax per licensed user per month, billed annually. Finance can calculate the annual subscription directly from the planned number of licences.

Moving from 8 to 15 licensed users increases the annual Tourvia subscription from €2,880 to €5,400 excluding tax. Buyers can include that change in the rollout budget without relying on an org-wide flat-price assumption.

How other alternatives compare on price

Salesforce Maps is not the only per-user option on the market. Other tools aimed at field teams follow similar models:

  • Badger Maps -- approximately $58/user/month, varying by plan
  • LeadBeam, $49 to $99/user/month depending on tier

Calculate the annual cost at the number of users who will plan or execute visits. An entry price for a smaller scope is not a comparable total.
